How Prayatn is governed

Three bodies — a Board of Trustees, a School Management Committee and a Parent Teacher Association — keep the organisation answerable, first of all to the community it serves.

Board of TrusteesSchool Management CommitteeParent Teacher AssociationTrust detailsTransparency
Board of Trustees

Seven members. Meets every quarter.

Board of Trustees

The Board of Trustees, consisting of seven members, manages the affairs of Prayatn. A meeting is held every quarter to analyse the performance of the organisation — including the role and functioning of the faculty and staff — to give suggestions, and to monitor the progress and achievements in tune with the plan of action of each project.

In an organisation of this size, a quarterly review is not a formality. It is where each project is measured against what it said it would do, where difficulties are raised while they are still small, and where the trustees decide what the coming quarter should look like.

School Management Committee

Fifteen members, including parents’ representatives.

School Management Committee

A fifteen-member School Management Committee has been constituted, comprising parents’ representatives, teachers, trustees and members from educational institutions. Three School Management Committee meetings were held to review the progress of the school and to develop a plan of action in tune with that progress.

The composition is deliberate. Parents bring what they see at home; teachers bring what they see in the classroom; trustees carry responsibility for the organisation as a whole; and members from educational institutions bring a view from outside Prayatn. Decisions taken by a group like that are harder to get wrong.

Parent Teacher Association

Parents, teachers and management together.

Parent Teacher Association

Prayatn has a parent-teacher association — a formal organisation composed of parents, teachers and management — to encourage parent participation in school activities. The aim of the PTA is to promote the all-round education of children and to update parents and teachers on the school’s activities and its progress.

The PTA is where the ordinary business of a school gets discussed: attendance, homework, what is happening at home, what parents are worried about. For parents who did not themselves finish school, being asked their opinion in a formal setting is itself part of what Prayatn is for.

Transparency

What we will show you, and how to ask.

Ask us anything

Small organisations earn trust by being easy to check. If you are considering supporting Prayatn, or you simply want to know how we work, you are welcome to ask for our annual report and audited accounts, to visit the school and the health centre, and to talk to the trustees.

The most useful thing you can do is come and see. Write or call and we will arrange a time.

Prayatn runs on people who decided to show up

An hour a week, a professional skill, a child’s school year, a box of books — there is a way for you to be part of this.
