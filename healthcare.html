Why healthcare

Distance and cost are what turn small illnesses into large ones.

Care that does not require a journey

Healthcare is fundamentally important for individual well-being and societal prosperity — ensuring longer, more fulfilling lives and a productive workforce. That is true at the level of a country, and it is true inside a single household, where one untreated illness can undo years of slow progress.

For families with very little, the barrier is rarely a lack of medicine. It is distance, cost and time. A visit to a distant hospital costs a day’s wages before anyone is even seen. So people wait, borrow, self-medicate, and arrive far later than they should have. The illness that could have been handled in ten minutes becomes the one that empties the savings.

Prayatn’s answer is to put care where the people are: a health centre in the neighbourhood, and a health programme inside the schools.

Swaasthya Kendra

Our community health centre.

Swaasthya Kendra

Swaasthya Kendra is Prayatn’s community health centre — the place a family in the neighbourhood can walk into when someone is unwell, without first calculating what the trip will cost.

Most of what a health centre like this does is unremarkable, and that is exactly the point: consultations, common illnesses, medicines, advice a family can trust, and a judgement about when something needs to be taken further. Care of that kind, available nearby and without fuss, prevents a great deal of avoidable suffering.

It also means somebody local knows these families’ medical histories — which is worth more than any single consultation.

School Health Program

Health care taken into the classroom.

School Health Program

The School Health Program takes health care to children where they already are. It provides regular health check-ups, awareness sessions and follow-up care for school children — so that being unwell never quietly becomes the reason a child stops attending.

Screening children in school reaches precisely those who would otherwise never be brought to a doctor: the ones whose parents cannot take time off, or who do not realise anything is wrong. A child does not report failing eyesight. They simply stop being able to follow the lesson, and are marked down as inattentive.

Awareness matters as much as examination. Handwashing, nutrition, adolescent health and hygiene are the sort of everyday knowledge that spreads from children back into their households.

Where a child needs treatment beyond what we can provide, they are guided towards it — not simply handed a referral slip.

How we work

Close by, continuous and joined up with the school.

Three things we try to get right

Proximity. Care that requires a journey is care that many families will not use. Everything we run is close enough to walk to.

Continuity. A one-off health camp produces photographs. A centre that is open week after week produces health. We have chosen the second.

Connection to the school. Because Prayatn runs both a school and a health service, a problem noticed in a classroom can be dealt with rather than merely observed. Very few organisations of our size can do this, and it is one of the most useful things about us.

How to help

Medical time, medicines, equipment.

How you can support this work

  • Doctors, nurses and counsellors who can give regular time are the single most valuable contribution to this work.
  • Medicines and medical supplies, and equipment for the health centre.
  • Fund the School Health Program for a year, or the treatment of children it identifies.
  • Health awareness sessions — if you can teach nutrition, hygiene or adolescent health well, we would welcome you.
