Our story

Founded in 1992. Still working in the same neighbourhoods.

Thirty years in one place

Prayatn was founded in 1992 as a voluntary effort to work with the disadvantaged sections of society, and it has been at that work ever since. It is a registered Public Charitable Trust based at Kalkaji, in South Delhi.

Organisations that last this long in one neighbourhood end up with something that cannot be built quickly: people know them. Families who came as children now send their own children. Parents will say what they actually think at a committee meeting. A woman who needs help knows there is a door she can knock on, and roughly what she will find behind it. That accumulated trust is the real asset of a small organisation, and it is why we have never wanted to be a large one.

Over the years the work has grown into three connected areas — education, healthcare and women’s development — and five projects running today. It has grown outward from what families told us they needed, rather than from a plan drawn up elsewhere.

What we believe

The convictions the work rests on.

Partners, not beneficiaries

The sentence we keep returning to is this: everyone should be an equal and active partner in the journey of development. It sounds like a slogan until you notice how much charitable work quietly assumes the opposite — that the people being helped are a problem to be managed rather than adults with judgement about their own lives.

We believe that capability matters more than relief. Giving a family something they need today is sometimes necessary, but it changes nothing on its own. Education, skills and health are what allow a household to stop depending on anyone’s goodwill, including ours.

We believe a dignified and independent life is the point. Not attendance figures, not the number of programmes run — whether a young person can earn, decide and hold their ground.

And we believe in staying. Development work does not happen in project cycles. It happens over the fifteen years it takes a child to grow up.

How we work

Six things that shape everything we do

We work inside the community, not at a distance from it

Our office, our school and our health centre are in the same neighbourhood as the families we serve. People can walk in. That single fact changes who actually gets help.

The people affected sit on the committees

Parents’ representatives are part of the School Management Committee that reviews our school and sets its plan of action. The Parent Teacher Association keeps that involvement continuous rather than ceremonial.

We follow through rather than refer on

A check-up that finds a problem is only useful if somebody makes sure the child is treated. A woman who needs to deal with an institution needs someone to go with her. Following through is most of the work.

Each project has a plan, and the plan is reviewed

The Board of Trustees meets every quarter to analyse the performance of the organisation — including the role and functioning of the faculty and staff — and to monitor progress against each project’s plan of action.

We treat health and education as one problem

A child who cannot see the blackboard is not a slow learner. A mother in an unsafe home cannot keep a child in school. Running these programmes under one roof is what lets us notice the connection.

We stay small enough to know people by name

Prayatn has never tried to scale into something that measures its work only in numbers. Knowing the families is what makes the work effective.

Where we work

Kalkaji and the neighbourhoods around it, in South Delhi.

Kalkaji, South Delhi

Prayatn works in and around Kalkaji in South Delhi, from an office at E-103, Ground Floor, Kalkaji, New Delhi – 110019. It is a part of the city where considerable wealth and considerable hardship live within a few hundred metres of each other — and where a family’s access to a decent school or a doctor depends almost entirely on which side of that line they were born.

Working in one area for three decades has a particular advantage. We are not guessing about what these families need, and they are not guessing about whether we will still be here next year.

Visitors are welcome — do call ahead so that someone is free to show you around.

Governance

A Board of seven trustees, a School Management Committee and a Parent Teacher Association.

Who is accountable, and to whom

Prayatn is managed by a Board of Trustees of seven members, which meets every quarter to analyse the organisation’s performance, give suggestions, and monitor progress and achievements against the plan of action for each project.

The school is overseen by a fifteen-member School Management Committee comprising parents’ representatives, teachers, trustees and members from educational institutions. Alongside it, a Parent Teacher Association brings parents, teachers and management together to encourage parent participation and to keep everyone informed about the school’s activities and progress.

The point of all three bodies is the same: the people most affected by a decision should have a seat where it is made.
