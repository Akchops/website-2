Who we are

A voluntary organisation and registered Public Charitable Trust, based in Kalkaji, New Delhi.

A developmental effort that has stayed in one place long enough to matter

Prayatn is a voluntary organisation that has worked with disadvantaged communities in and around Kalkaji, New Delhi since 1992. We are a registered Public Charitable Trust. Everything we do is directed at one end: that a family which begins with very little should not be trapped there, and that the people in these neighbourhoods should be equal and active partners in their own development rather than recipients of somebody else’s plan for them.

In practice that has meant staying with the same communities year after year, through three connected kinds of work. We run a school and a scholarship scheme, because education is what changes what the next generation believes is possible. We run a community health centre and a school health programme, because a family that cannot reach a doctor loses its footing quickly. And we provide care and support to women survivors of gender based violence, because safety and dignity are the ground everything else stands on.

None of this is delivered from a distance. Our office and our work are in the same neighbourhood as the people we serve. Parents sit on the committee that runs our school. Our health programmes go into classrooms rather than waiting for families to find us. The organisation is small, and it is deliberately close to the ground.

Join us in creating a society where everyone is an equal and active partner in the journey of development.

Prayatn is governed by a Board of seven trustees, supported by a fifteen-member School Management Committee and an active Parent Teacher Association. We work with partners — foundations, companies and institutions — who fund and strengthen particular parts of this work.

Our projects

What Prayatn is running today

Five projects, each with its own plan of action, reviewed by the Board of Trustees every quarter.

Healthcare

Swaasthya Kendra

Our community health centre. Swaasthya Kendra exists so that ordinary, everyday medical care — a consultation, a prescription, advice a family can trust — is available in the neighbourhood rather than at the end of a long and expensive journey.

For families living close to the edge, that distance is what turns a small illness into a serious one. A parent who would lose a day’s wages to visit a hospital will often simply wait. Having care within walking distance changes the arithmetic entirely.

Healthcare

School Health Program

Health care taken into the classroom. Through regular check-ups, awareness sessions and follow-up care, the School Health Program reaches children who are the least likely to be brought to a doctor in time.

Much of what it finds is ordinary and fixable — eyesight, nutrition, skin and dental problems, untreated infections. Left alone, these are exactly the things that keep a child at the back of the class, or out of it. Children who need further treatment are guided towards it rather than simply referred and forgotten.

Education

Seth Vidyalaya

Our school. Seth Vidyalaya is where children from the community learn, play and are prepared for the world beyond the classroom — many of them the first in their family to be schooled at all.

The school is run with the community, not merely for it. A fifteen-member School Management Committee — parents’ representatives, teachers, trustees and members from educational institutions — reviews its progress and develops its plan of action, and a Parent Teacher Association keeps parents involved in what happens day to day.

Education

Scholarship Scheme for Meritorious Students

Support for bright students so that money is never the reason a promising child stops studying. The scheme exists for the moment families most often run out of resources — when a capable student needs to go further than the household can carry.

Women Development

Addressing Gender Based Violence in Communities

Comprehensive care and support to women survivors of gender based violence. The work combines direct support for individual women with awareness and prevention efforts in the community around them.

Violence rarely ends with a single incident, and leaving it is rarely a single decision. What women need is somewhere close by to turn to, someone who will stay with them through the practical steps, and the means to stand on their own afterwards.
