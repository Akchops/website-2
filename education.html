Why education

The first thing a family loses, and the thing that changes everything.

School is rarely a given

For a child growing up in a low-income neighbourhood, education is not automatic. Fees, distance, a household that needs another pair of hands earning, an older sibling who must be looked after, a parent who never went to school and cannot help with homework — any one of these can end a child’s education before it has properly started. None of them has anything to do with how capable the child is.

Prayatn’s education work exists to remove those obstacles one at a time, and to keep removing them for as long as it takes. That means a school the community can actually reach; support at the point where families run out of money; and parents kept close enough to the school to be part of it rather than merely informed by it.

Every child who finishes school changes what the next generation of that family believes is possible. That is not a sentimental claim — it is the most reliable thing we have seen in thirty years of this work.

Seth Vidyalaya

Our school in the community.

Seth Vidyalaya

Seth Vidyalaya is Prayatn’s school: a place in the middle of the community where children learn, play and are prepared for the world beyond the classroom. For many of them it is the first formal education anyone in their family has had.

A school of this kind has to do more than teach. It has to be somewhere a child is safe for the length of a day, where a teacher notices when something is wrong at home, and where a routine holds steady even when everything around it does not. Families rely on that steadiness as much as they rely on the lessons.

The school is run with the community rather than merely for it. A fifteen-member School Management Committee — comprising parents’ representatives, teachers, trustees and members from educational institutions — reviews the school’s progress and develops its plan of action.

If you would like to see the school, please get in touch and arrange a visit.

Scholarships

For students who would otherwise have to stop.

Scholarship Scheme for Meritorious Students

There is a particular moment when families run out of resources: a student is doing well, the next stage of education costs more than the household can carry, and the sensible decision — the one taken in thousands of homes every year — is for the young person to stop studying and start earning.

The scholarship scheme exists for that moment. It supports meritorious students so that money is never the reason a promising child stops. It is deliberately aimed at the students most likely to be lost precisely because they were capable enough to keep going.

If you would like to fund a student through a year of study, see ways to help.

Parents & committees

Parents at the table, not in the waiting room.

Parents are part of how the school is run

Two bodies keep the school answerable to the families who use it.

The School Management Committee has fifteen members — parents’ representatives, teachers, trustees and members from educational institutions. It meets to review the school’s progress and to develop a plan of action in tune with that progress.

The Parent Teacher Association is a formal body of parents, teachers and management, formed to encourage parent participation in school activities. Its aim is to promote the all-round education of children and to keep parents and teachers updated on the school’s activities and its progress.

For parents who did not themselves finish school, sitting on a committee that governs their child’s education is not a small thing. It is part of what we mean by equal and active partners.

How to help

Fund a student, teach a class, give materials.

How you can support this work

  • Support a student through a year of school, or contribute to the scholarship scheme.
  • Teach or tutor — regular volunteers who come every week make far more difference than occasional visitors.
  • Coach for exams if you can help older students through the stages where they are most likely to drop out.
  • Give materials — books, stationery, uniforms, computers and teaching material are always needed and always used.
